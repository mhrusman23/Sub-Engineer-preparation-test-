Q1. The first step of a risk assessment is to:

  1. A. Write a report
  2. B. Identify the hazards
  3. C. Buy PPE
  4. D. Train workers
Answer: B. Identify the hazards

Q2. A risk assessment should be reviewed:

  1. A. Never
  2. B. When there is significant change or after an incident
  3. C. Every 10 years only
  4. D. Only by inspectors
Answer: B. When there is significant change or after an incident

Q3. The hierarchy of control starts with:

  1. A. PPE
  2. B. Elimination of the hazard
  3. C. Warning signs
  4. D. Training
Answer: B. Elimination of the hazard

Q4. Which control is an example of an engineering control?

  1. A. Gloves
  2. B. Machine guarding / ventilation
  3. C. A safety talk
  4. D. A poster
Answer: B. Machine guarding / ventilation

Q5. A 5x5 risk matrix combines:

  1. A. Cost and profit
  2. B. Likelihood and severity scores
  3. C. Time and money
  4. D. Height and weight
Answer: B. Likelihood and severity scores

Q6. 'Suitable and sufficient' risk assessment means it is:

  1. A. Very long
  2. B. Appropriate to the level of risk
  3. C. Written by a lawyer
  4. D. Kept secret
Answer: B. Appropriate to the level of risk

Q7. Who should be involved in carrying out risk assessments?

  1. A. Only the director
  2. B. Competent people including the workers doing the job
  3. C. Only visitors
  4. D. Only the newest staff
Answer: B. Competent people including the workers doing the job

Q8. Residual risk is:

  1. A. The risk before controls
  2. B. The risk remaining after controls are applied
  3. C. A type of insurance
  4. D. Zero risk
Answer: B. The risk remaining after controls are applied

Q9. A dynamic risk assessment is:

  1. A. Done once a year
  2. B. Carried out on the spot as conditions change
  3. C. Done by computer only
  4. D. Never documented
Answer: B. Carried out on the spot as conditions change

Q10. Recording significant findings of a risk assessment is required when a company has:

  1. A. Any number of workers
  2. B. Five or more employees (in UK law)
  3. C. Only one worker
  4. D. Over 500 workers
Answer: B. Five or more employees (in UK law)

Q11. What is the primary legal and moral purpose of recording the significant findings of a risk assessment?

  1. A. To prove that work can continue without stopping
  2. B. To provide a clear record of hazards, risks, and control measures for workers and inspectors
  3. C. To calculate the annual company tax rebates
  4. D. To eliminate all potential hazards permanently
Answer: B. To provide a clear record of hazards, risks, and control measures for workers and inspectors

Q12. According to general health and safety principles, when must a risk assessment be reviewed?

  1. A. Only when a fatal accident occurs
  2. B. Every ten years without exception
  3. C. When there is a significant change in work practices or equipment
  4. D. Only if ordered by an external inspector
Answer: C. When there is a significant change in work practices or equipment

Q13. Which of the following best describes 'residual risk'?

  1. A. The risk remaining after all control measures have been implemented
  2. B. The initial risk before any safety action is taken
  3. C. The risk caused by failing to hire enough staff
  4. D. The acceptable risk level defined by the government
Answer: A. The risk remaining after all control measures have been implemented

Q14. Which measure represents an administrative control in the hierarchy of risk control?

  1. A. Fitting a guard on a rotating machine
  2. B. Providing earplugs to machine operators
  3. C. Rotating workers to limit their exposure time to noise
  4. D. Replacing a toxic solvent with water-based soap
Answer: C. Rotating workers to limit their exposure time to noise

Q15. What does the term 'likelihood' refer to in the context of risk assessment?

  1. A. The total cost of an accident
  2. B. The chance or probability that harm will occur
  3. C. The number of workers on site
  4. D. The severity of the injury
Answer: B. The chance or probability that harm will occur

Q16. When assessing risks in a workplace, who should ideally be consulted during the process?

  1. A. Only external health and safety consultants
  2. B. Only the company board of directors
  3. C. The workers doing the actual job and their representatives
  4. D. Only the company accountants
Answer: C. The workers doing the actual job and their representatives

Q17. What is the primary definition of a hazard in workplace safety management?

  1. A. The chance that someone will be harmed
  2. B. Anything with the potential to cause harm
  3. C. The severity of an injury
  4. D. The cost of an accident
Answer: B. Anything with the potential to cause harm

Q18. In safety terminology, what does the term 'risk' specifically represent?

  1. A. The physical source of danger
  2. B. The likelihood of harm combined with its severity
  3. C. The immediate cost of damage
  4. D. The number of workers exposed
Answer: B. The likelihood of harm combined with its severity

Q19. Which of the following is considered a personal protective equipment (PPE) control measure?

  1. A. Installing machine guards
  2. B. Rotating shift workers
  3. C. Wearing safety helmets
  4. D. Substituting toxic chemicals
Answer: C. Wearing safety helmets

Q20. What action is typically required when a risk assessment identifies an intolerable level of risk?

  1. A. Ignore the risk if production is high
  2. B. Implement immediate control measures to reduce the risk
  3. C. Wait for an annual review to address it
  4. D. Inform workers and continue normal operations
Answer: B. Implement immediate control measures to reduce the risk

Q21. Why is worker consultation essential during the risk assessment process?

  1. A. It speeds up paperwork completion
  2. B. Workers have practical daily insight into hidden hazards
  3. C. It eliminates all company liability
  4. D. It reduces the need for safety equipment
Answer: B. Workers have practical daily insight into hidden hazards

Q22. Which category of control measure involves changing the way work is organized or scheduled?

  1. A. Engineering control
  2. B. Administrative control
  3. C. Elimination
  4. D. Substitution
Answer: B. Administrative control

Q23. Which of the following represents an administrative control measure in occupational safety?

  1. A. Wearing earplugs
  2. B. Job rotation to reduce exposure time
  3. C. Replacing a toxic chemical with water
  4. D. Installing local exhaust ventilation
Answer: B. Job rotation to reduce exposure time

Q24. Which document type serves as an authoritative source of information when developing safe operating procedures?

  1. A. Approved Codes of Practice (ACOPs)
  2. B. Daily tabloid newspapers
  3. C. Fictional safety novels
  4. D. Personal diary entries
Answer: A. Approved Codes of Practice (ACOPs)

Q25. Which option best describes personal factors that can contribute to inadequate risk assessment or unsafe acts?

  1. A. Outdated office furniture
  2. B. Fatigue, stress, and the influence of substances
  3. C. High electricity tariffs
  4. D. Lack of decorative posters
Answer: B. Fatigue, stress, and the influence of substances
