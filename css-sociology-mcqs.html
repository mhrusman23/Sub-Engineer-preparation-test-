Q1. Sociology is the scientific study of:

  1. A. Rocks
  2. B. Human society and social behaviour
  3. C. Plants
  4. D. Markets only
Answer: B. Human society and social behaviour

Q2. Who is known as the father of sociology?

  1. A. Karl Marx
  2. B. Auguste Comte
  3. C. Max Weber
  4. D. Emile Durkheim
Answer: B. Auguste Comte

Q3. A 'norm' in society is:

  1. A. A law only
  2. B. A shared rule or expectation of behaviour
  3. C. A building
  4. D. A ritual object
Answer: B. A shared rule or expectation of behaviour

Q4. Socialisation is the process by which individuals:

  1. A. Earn money
  2. B. Learn the culture and norms of their society
  3. C. Move cities
  4. D. Form governments
Answer: B. Learn the culture and norms of their society

Q5. Durkheim's famous study 'Suicide' showed that suicide rates are influenced by:

  1. A. Weather only
  2. B. Social integration and regulation
  3. C. Income only
  4. D. Genetics only
Answer: B. Social integration and regulation

Q6. A nuclear family consists of:

  1. A. Grandparents and cousins
  2. B. Parents and their children
  3. C. Only siblings
  4. D. A whole clan
Answer: B. Parents and their children

Q7. Culture includes:

  1. A. Only language
  2. B. Beliefs, values, customs and material objects of a group
  3. C. Only food
  4. D. Only religion
Answer: B. Beliefs, values, customs and material objects of a group

Q8. Urbanisation refers to:

  1. A. Building roads
  2. B. The growth of cities and city living
  3. C. Farming
  4. D. Migration to villages
Answer: B. The growth of cities and city living

Q9. Deviance in sociology means behaviour that:

  1. A. Is always criminal
  2. B. Violates social norms
  3. C. Is always punished
  4. D. Is rare
Answer: B. Violates social norms

Q10. Primary groups (like family) are characterised by:

  1. A. Formal rules
  2. B. Close, personal and long-lasting relationships
  3. C. Large size
  4. D. Written contracts
Answer: B. Close, personal and long-lasting relationships

Q11. Which theoretical perspective views society as a complex system whose parts work together to promote solidarity and stability?

  1. A. Conflict Theory
  2. B. Structural Functionalism
  3. C. Symbolic Interactionism
  4. D. Exchange Theory
Answer: B. Structural Functionalism

Q12. Max Weber introduced which concept to describe a detached, objective understanding of social behavior from the actor's viewpoint?

  1. A. Anomie
  2. B. Verstehen
  3. C. Bourgeoisie
  4. D. Habitus
Answer: B. Verstehen

Q13. What term did Karl Marx use to describe the social class that owns the means of production in a capitalist society?

  1. A. Proletariat
  2. B. Bourgeoisie
  3. C. Lumpenproletariat
  4. D. Peasantry
Answer: B. Bourgeoisie

Q14. Which term describes a society's temporary adoption of new styles, behaviors, or modes of dress that quickly rise and fall in popularity?

  1. A. Custom
  2. B. Fad
  3. C. Mores
  4. D. Taboo
Answer: B. Fad

Q15. According to Charles Horton Cooley, the concept of the 'looking-glass self' suggests that our self-identity is formed through:

  1. A. Genetic inheritance
  2. B. Perceptions of how others view us
  3. C. Economic status
  4. D. Formal educational degrees
Answer: B. Perceptions of how others view us

Q16. What sociological term refers to the movement of individuals or groups between different social positions or strata?

  1. A. Social stratification
  2. B. Social mobility
  3. C. Social integration
  4. D. Social differentiation
Answer: B. Social mobility

Q17. In Karl Marx's theory of social stratification, the bourgeoisie refers to the social class that:

  1. A. Owns the means of production
  2. B. Sells its wage labor to survive
  3. C. Performs agricultural subsistence farming
  4. D. Governs the religious institutions
Answer: A. Owns the means of production

Q18. The lifelong social experience by which individuals develop their human potential and learn culture is known as:

  1. A. Assimilation
  2. B. Socialisation
  3. C. Stratification
  4. D. Secularisation
Answer: B. Socialisation

Q19. Max Weber used which German term to describe empathetic understanding and interpretation of human social action?

  1. A. Gemeinschaft
  2. B. Gesellschaft
  3. C. Verstehen
  4. D. Anomie
Answer: C. Verstehen

Q20. Emile Durkheim's sociological analysis of suicide highlighted the strong influence of which factor?

  1. A. Genetic predisposition
  2. B. Individual psychological trauma
  3. C. Social integration and regulation
  4. D. Dietary habits
Answer: C. Social integration and regulation

Q21. Which term refers to behaviour that violates significant social norms and expectations?

  1. A. Conformity
  2. B. Deviance
  3. C. Altruism
  4. D. Assimilation
Answer: B. Deviance

Q22. Secondary groups are typically distinguished from primary groups by being:

  1. A. Small and emotionally intense
  2. B. Goal-oriented and impersonal
  3. C. Hereditary and lifelong
  4. D. Based exclusively on kinship
Answer: B. Goal-oriented and impersonal

Q23. Who introduced the concept of the 'sociological imagination' to describe the ability to connect individual experiences to larger societal forces?

  1. A. C. Wright Mills
  2. B. Auguste Comte
  3. C. Herbert Spencer
  4. D. Talcott Parsons
Answer: A. C. Wright Mills

Q24. According to Robert K. Merton's strain theory, what mode of adaptation describes individuals who reject both culturally approved goals and the institutionalized means to achieve them, often dropping out of conventional society?

  1. A. Innovation
  2. B. Ritualism
  3. C. Retreatism
  4. D. Rebellion
Answer: C. Retreatism

Q25. Which term describes the process of learning and internalizing the values, beliefs, and norms of one's culture?

  1. A. Stratification
  2. B. Socialization
  3. C. Assimilation
  4. D. Secularization
Answer: B. Socialization
