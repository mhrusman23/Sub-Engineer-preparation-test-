Q1. How many parts are in the IELTS Speaking test?

  1. A. Two
  2. B. Three
  3. C. Four
  4. D. Five
Answer: B. Three

Q2. In Part 2 (the long turn), you speak for:

  1. A. 30 seconds
  2. B. 1 to 2 minutes
  3. C. 5 minutes
  4. D. 10 minutes
Answer: B. 1 to 2 minutes

Q3. Before the Part 2 long turn, you get preparation time of:

  1. A. No time
  2. B. 1 minute
  3. C. 5 minutes
  4. D. 10 minutes
Answer: B. 1 minute

Q4. Part 1 questions are about:

  1. A. Advanced science
  2. B. Familiar topics like home and family
  3. C. Politics only
  4. D. Business law
Answer: B. Familiar topics like home and family

Q5. Fluency means:

  1. A. Speaking very fast
  2. B. Speaking smoothly without long pauses
  3. C. Using only long words
  4. D. Speaking loudly
Answer: B. Speaking smoothly without long pauses

Q6. In Speaking, grammatical range means:

  1. A. Using one tense only
  2. B. Using a variety of sentence structures
  3. C. Avoiding all errors
  4. D. Speaking in questions
Answer: B. Using a variety of sentence structures

Q7. The Speaking test lasts about:

  1. A. 5 minutes
  2. B. 11 to 14 minutes
  3. C. 30 minutes
  4. D. 45 minutes
Answer: B. 11 to 14 minutes

Q8. If you do not understand a question, you should:

  1. A. Stay silent
  2. B. Politely ask the examiner to repeat it
  3. C. Answer a different question
  4. D. Leave the room
Answer: B. Politely ask the examiner to repeat it

Q9. Part 3 questions are:

  1. A. Yes/no only
  2. B. More abstract discussion linked to Part 2
  3. C. Spelling tests
  4. D. Reading aloud
Answer: B. More abstract discussion linked to Part 2

Q10. Your accent in IELTS Speaking:

  1. A. Must be British
  2. B. Must be American
  3. C. Does not matter if you are clear
  4. D. Gives extra marks
Answer: C. Does not matter if you are clear

Q11. Which of the following best describes the type of language used in Part 3 of the IELTS Speaking test?

  1. A. Simple, everyday phrases only
  2. B. Highly technical jargon from scientific fields
  3. C. More abstract and academic vocabulary related to the topic
  4. D. Only questions in the passive voice
Answer: C. More abstract and academic vocabulary related to the topic

Q12. What is the primary purpose of the follow‑up questions in Part 3 of the IELTS Speaking test?

  1. A. To repeat the same question in a different language
  2. B. To ask the candidate to write a short paragraph
  3. C. To encourage a deeper discussion on the topic introduced in Part 2
  4. D. To test the candidate’s reading comprehension skills
Answer: C. To encourage a deeper discussion on the topic introduced in Part 2

Q13. Which of the following best describes the main aim of Part 1 in the IELTS Speaking test?

  1. A. To evaluate the candidate’s ability to write a short essay
  2. B. To assess the candidate’s knowledge of advanced scientific terminology
  3. C. To gather information about the candidate’s everyday life and interests
  4. D. To test the candidate’s ability to translate complex sentences
Answer: C. To gather information about the candidate’s everyday life and interests

Q14. During the IELTS Speaking test, how long does the examiner typically allow for the long turn in Part 2?

  1. A. 30 seconds
  2. B. 1 minute
  3. C. 2 minutes
  4. D. 3 minutes
Answer: C. 2 minutes

Q15. In the IELTS Speaking test, what does 'fluency' refer to?

  1. A. Speaking with a strong regional accent
  2. B. Using only complex vocabulary
  3. C. Speaking smoothly without unnecessary pauses
  4. D. Reading aloud without hesitation
Answer: C. Speaking smoothly without unnecessary pauses

Q16. What is the primary objective of Part 3 in the IELTS Speaking test?

  1. A. To talk about personal childhood memories
  2. B. To discuss abstract ideas and broader social issues
  3. C. To read a passage aloud to the examiner
  4. D. To describe a physical object on the desk
Answer: B. To discuss abstract ideas and broader social issues

Q17. How is lexical resource assessed in the IELTS Speaking evaluation criteria?

  1. A. By how loudly and clearly you project your voice
  2. B. By your ability to use a wide range of vocabulary flexibly and accurately
  3. C. By the speed at which you finish your long turn
  4. D. By your ability to avoid using any idioms at all
Answer: B. By your ability to use a wide range of vocabulary flexibly and accurately

Q18. What should you do if you finish speaking before the examiner stops you in Part 2?

  1. A. Sit in complete silence until the time expires
  2. B. Ask the examiner for the correct answer
  3. C. Expand on your points or add relevant details until stopped
  4. D. Apologize and immediately walk out of the room
Answer: C. Expand on your points or add relevant details until stopped

Q19. Which of the following best characterizes pronunciation in the IELTS Speaking test?

  1. A. Having a native British or American accent is strictly mandatory
  2. B. Using natural sentence stress, intonation, and being easily understood
  3. C. Speaking in a robotic and perfectly steady monotone
  4. D. Pronouncing every single silent letter with extra emphasis
Answer: B. Using natural sentence stress, intonation, and being easily understood

Q20. What kind of interaction takes place during Part 1 of the IELTS Speaking test?

  1. A. A debate between two test-takers
  2. B. A structured interview with familiar everyday topics
  3. C. A formal lecture delivered by the candidate
  4. D. A role-play negotiation scenario
Answer: B. A structured interview with familiar everyday topics

Q21. Why is coherence important in your spoken responses?

  1. A. It ensures your ideas are logically organized and easy to follow
  2. B. It guarantees you will speak without ever pausing
  3. C. It allows you to use complex grammar without making mistakes
  4. D. It forces the examiner to give you the highest possible band score
Answer: A. It ensures your ideas are logically organized and easy to follow

Q22. In the IELTS Speaking test, how is the duration of Part 2 structured regarding the candidate's solo talk?

  1. A. Up to 2 minutes maximum
  2. B. Exactly 30 seconds
  3. C. Between 4 and 5 minutes
  4. D. At least 5 minutes
Answer: A. Up to 2 minutes maximum

Q23. What kind of performance best demonstrates lexical resource in the speaking test?

  1. A. Using a wide vocabulary accurately and naturally, including less common phrases
  2. B. Repeating the exact words from the examiner's prompt multiple times
  3. C. Relying exclusively on basic primary school vocabulary
  4. D. Translating every word literally from another language
Answer: A. Using a wide vocabulary accurately and naturally, including less common phrases

Q24. How are the questions in Part 3 of the IELTS Speaking test generally linked to the preceding section?

  1. A. They ask for spelling of keywords from Part 2
  2. B. They focus entirely on random unrelated riddles
  3. C. They require reading a written paragraph aloud
  4. D. They involve broader, more abstract discussions related to the Part 2 theme
Answer: D. They involve broader, more abstract discussions related to the Part 2 theme

Q25. Which of the following best describes the overall format of the IELTS Speaking module?

  1. A. A group discussion with multiple test-takers
  2. B. A face-to-face interview conducted by a certified examiner
  3. C. An automated computer-based recording session with no human present
  4. D. A written questionnaire answered verbally into a microphone
Answer: B. A face-to-face interview conducted by a certified examiner
