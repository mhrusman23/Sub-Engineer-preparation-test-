Q1. Electricity can kill at voltages as low as:

  1. A. 1000 V
  2. B. 50 V in wet conditions
  3. C. 10,000 V
  4. D. It cannot kill
Answer: B. 50 V in wet conditions

Q2. The main effects of electric shock include:

  1. A. Burns and stopping the heart
  2. B. Hearing loss
  3. C. Back pain
  4. D. Skin rash only
Answer: A. Burns and stopping the heart

Q3. An RCD (residual current device) protects by:

  1. A. Increasing voltage
  2. B. Cutting power quickly when current leaks to earth
  3. C. Making cables stronger
  4. D. Cooling equipment
Answer: B. Cutting power quickly when current leaks to earth

Q4. PAT testing refers to checking:

  1. A. Buildings
  2. B. Portable electrical appliances
  3. C. Vehicles
  4. D. Lifts
Answer: B. Portable electrical appliances

Q5. Before working on electrical equipment, you must:

  1. A. Speed up
  2. B. Isolate it and prove it is dead
  3. C. Wet your hands
  4. D. Remove the fuse box
Answer: B. Isolate it and prove it is dead

Q6. Overloading a socket can cause:

  1. A. Savings
  2. B. Overheating and fire
  3. C. Better performance
  4. D. Nothing
Answer: B. Overheating and fire

Q7. Only competent electricians should:

  1. A. Use a kettle
  2. B. Carry out electrical installation and repairs
  3. C. Switch lights on
  4. D. Plug in a charger
Answer: B. Carry out electrical installation and repairs

Q8. Damaged cables should be:

  1. A. Taped and used
  2. B. Removed from service immediately
  3. C. Painted
  4. D. Used outdoors only
Answer: B. Removed from service immediately

Q9. In wet conditions, electrical risk is:

  1. A. Lower
  2. B. Higher because water conducts electricity
  3. C. The same
  4. D. Zero
Answer: B. Higher because water conducts electricity

Q10. A fuse protects a circuit by:

  1. A. Melting and breaking the circuit when current is too high
  2. B. Increasing current
  3. C. Storing electricity
  4. D. Making light
Answer: A. Melting and breaking the circuit when current is too high

Q11. Which type of ladder material is strictly recommended to avoid electrical conductivity when working near energized overhead power lines?

  1. A. Aluminum
  2. B. Steel
  3. C. Fiberglass
  4. D. Galvanized iron
Answer: C. Fiberglass

Q12. Why can relying solely on personal protective equipment (PPE) like rubber insulating gloves be hazardous over long periods?

  1. A. PPE permanently increases electrical resistance infinitely
  2. B. Material degradation from heat, UV exposure, and wear reduces its protective capability
  3. C. Insulating gloves make tools completely weightless and hard to handle
  4. D. PPE automatically regenerates itself after each use
Answer: B. Material degradation from heat, UV exposure, and wear reduces its protective capability

Q13. The severity and physiological impact of an electrical shock on the human body depend on several factors, EXCEPT which of the following?

  1. A. The color of the outer jacket of the cable
  2. B. The current magnitude flowing through the body
  3. C. The exact pathway the current takes across organs
  4. D. The total duration of time the body is exposed
Answer: A. The color of the outer jacket of the cable

Q14. What should a worker do if they discover a portable power drill with a frayed power cord and a broken casing?

  1. A. Use insulating tape to wrap the cord and continue working
  2. B. Tag the equipment out of service and report it to the maintenance department
  3. C. Plug it into a different socket to see if it functions normally
  4. D. Store it back in the general tool rack for others to use
Answer: B. Tag the equipment out of service and report it to the maintenance department

Q15. What is the primary action a worker should take immediately if a colleague suffers a severe electric shock and remains in contact with the live source?

  1. A. Pull the victim away using bare hands immediately
  2. B. Disconnect the power source safely before touching the victim
  3. C. Throw water on the electrical equipment to cool it down
  4. D. Leave the area to find a supervisor without checking the victim
Answer: B. Disconnect the power source safely before touching the victim

Q16. Which of the following common environmental mediums acts as a natural conductor of electricity?

  1. A. Dry air
  2. B. Pure rubber
  3. C. Moist concrete
  4. D. Dry glass
Answer: C. Moist concrete

Q17. What is the primary function of Lockout/Tagout (LOTO) procedures during electrical maintenance?

  1. A. To speed up repair times
  2. B. To isolate hazardous energy and prevent accidental startup
  3. C. To test circuit breaker sensitivity
  4. D. To ground excess static electricity
Answer: B. To isolate hazardous energy and prevent accidental startup

Q18. Which safety device is specifically designed to guard against arc flash hazards by quickly clearing high-current faults?

  1. A. Standard household light switch
  2. B. Arc fault circuit interrupter (AFCI)
  3. C. Insulated rubber mat
  4. D. Step-down transformer
Answer: B. Arc fault circuit interrupter (AFCI)

Q19. Why are wooden or fiberglass ladders preferred over aluminum ladders during electrical work?

  1. A. They are lighter to carry
  2. B. They do not conduct electricity
  3. C. They are cheaper to purchase
  4. D. They hold more weight
Answer: B. They do not conduct electricity

Q20. What does a dielectric test performed on insulating rubber gloves verify?

  1. A. Their physical weight
  2. B. Their resistance to high voltage breakthrough
  3. C. Their color fastness
  4. D. Their flexibility in cold weather
Answer: B. Their resistance to high voltage breakthrough

Q21. Which color is universally designated for grounding conductors in modern electrical wiring systems?

  1. A. Red
  2. B. Blue
  3. C. Green or Green with yellow stripes
  4. D. Black
Answer: C. Green or Green with yellow stripes

Q22. Which specific body tissue offers the highest electrical resistance when a person receives a shock?

  1. A. Blood
  2. B. Muscle
  3. C. Bone
  4. D. Nerves
Answer: C. Bone

Q23. What does an IP (Ingress Protection) rating primarily indicate about an electrical enclosure?

  1. A. Maximum operating voltage level
  2. B. Protection against dust and water ingress
  3. C. Maximum allowable cable gauge size
  4. D. Continuous current carrying capacity
Answer: B. Protection against dust and water ingress

Q24. What critical information is typically displayed on warning labels attached to electrical distribution panels?

  1. A. Company decorative logos
  2. B. Voltage levels and potential hazards
  3. C. The exact age of the building
  4. D. Interior paint color codes
Answer: B. Voltage levels and potential hazards

Q25. What is the primary function of performing an insulation resistance test on electrical cables?

  1. A. Measure total voltage output
  2. B. Verify the effectiveness and integrity of insulation
  3. C. Measure local earth resistance only
  4. D. Check the exact color coding of wires
Answer: B. Verify the effectiveness and integrity of insulation
