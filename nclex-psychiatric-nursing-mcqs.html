Q1. The priority when caring for a suicidal patient is:

  1. A. Giving advice
  2. B. Ensuring safety and constant observation
  3. C. Leaving them alone
  4. D. Arguing with them
Answer: B. Ensuring safety and constant observation

Q2. Therapeutic communication includes:

  1. A. Giving false reassurance
  2. B. Active listening and open-ended questions
  3. C. Talking about yourself
  4. D. Ignoring feelings
Answer: B. Active listening and open-ended questions

Q3. A patient with schizophrenia may experience:

  1. A. Hallucinations and delusions
  2. B. Only physical pain
  3. C. Memory loss only
  4. D. Seizures
Answer: A. Hallucinations and delusions

Q4. Lithium is used to treat:

  1. A. Depression only
  2. B. Bipolar disorder
  3. C. Anxiety only
  4. D. Insomnia
Answer: B. Bipolar disorder

Q5. Signs of lithium toxicity include:

  1. A. Tremor, vomiting and confusion
  2. B. Hair growth
  3. C. Weight loss only
  4. D. Rash only
Answer: B. Hair growth

Q6. For an anxious patient, the nurse should:

  1. A. Use long explanations
  2. B. Stay calm, use short simple statements
  3. C. Raise their voice
  4. D. Leave immediately
Answer: B. Stay calm, use short simple statements

Q7. Depression is characterised by:

  1. A. Excess energy
  2. B. Persistent low mood and loss of interest
  3. C. Hallucinations always
  4. D. High fever
Answer: B. Persistent low mood and loss of interest

Q8. A patient refusing medication has the right to:

  1. A. Be forced always
  2. B. Refuse, except in specific legal emergencies
  3. C. Be punished
  4. D. Be discharged immediately
Answer: B. Refuse, except in specific legal emergencies

Q9. Delirium differs from dementia because delirium:

  1. A. Is permanent
  2. B. Starts suddenly and fluctuates
  3. C. Only affects the young
  4. D. Cannot be treated
Answer: B. Starts suddenly and fluctuates

Q10. The best response to an angry patient is to:

  1. A. Argue back
  2. B. Stay calm, listen and set clear limits
  3. C. Threaten them
  4. D. Walk away without a word
Answer: B. Stay calm, listen and set clear limits

Q11. A client diagnosed with schizophrenia is prescribed clozapine. Which mandatory laboratory monitoring protocol must the nurse explain to the client?

  1. A. Daily total cholesterol checks
  2. B. Periodic absolute neutrophil count (ANC) blood testing
  3. C. Weekly urinalysis for protein levels
  4. D. Monthly thyroid hormone panel testing
Answer: B. Periodic absolute neutrophil count (ANC) blood testing

Q12. A psychiatric nurse is reviewing common side effects associated with the initiation of various psychotropic medications. Which physical symptom is frequently reported by clients starting these regimens?

  1. A. Significant permanent height increase
  2. B. Gastrointestinal upset including nausea and vomiting
  3. C. Immediate and permanent visual acuity restoration
  4. D. Complete loss of the need for sleep
Answer: B. Gastrointestinal upset including nausea and vomiting

Q13. A nurse is caring for a patient experiencing acute alcohol withdrawal. Which medication class is typically administered first to prevent severe complications such as seizures?

  1. A. Atypical antipsychotics
  2. B. Benzodiazepines
  3. C. Tricyclic antidepressants
  4. D. Mood-stabilizing anticonvulsants only
Answer: B. Benzodiazepines

Q14. During a psychoeducational session on monoamine oxidase inhibitors (MAOIs), a client asks about dietary restrictions. Which food item must the nurse instruct the client to strictly avoid to prevent a hypertensive crisis?

  1. A. Fresh apples and bananas
  2. B. Aged cheeses and cured meats
  3. C. Plain boiled white rice
  4. D. Standard pasteurized whole milk
Answer: B. Aged cheeses and cured meats

Q15. When assessing a client who has been diagnosed with obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD), the nurse recognizes that compulsions are best defined as:

  1. A. Intrusive, unwanted thoughts that cause marked anxiety
  2. B. Repetitive behaviors or mental acts performed to reduce anxiety
  3. C. Delusional beliefs that lack any connection to reality
  4. D. Sudden periods of intense fear accompanied by physical symptoms
Answer: B. Repetitive behaviors or mental acts performed to reduce anxiety

Q16. When developing an initial nursing care plan for an adult patient admitted with severe clinical depression and expressions of total hopelessness, which intervention must receive the highest priority?

  1. A. Immediate participation in high-energy group activities
  2. B. Rigorous assessment of the patient's risk for self-harm or suicide
  3. C. Advising the patient to avoid all social interactions until mood improves
  4. D. Encouraging the patient to make major life decisions to boost morale
Answer: B. Rigorous assessment of the patient's risk for self-harm or suicide

Q17. When teaching a client about prolonged benzodiazepine use, which statement most accurately reflects the long‑term consequences?

  1. A. It will likely cause permanent liver damage.
  2. B. It may lead to tolerance and physical dependence.
  3. C. It can increase the risk of developing hypertension.
  4. D. It most commonly causes irreversible memory loss.
Answer: B. It may lead to tolerance and physical dependence.

Q18. A client with a history of bipolar disorder is receiving lithium therapy. Which laboratory value should the nurse most closely monitor to detect an early sign of toxicity?

  1. A. Serum sodium concentration
  2. B. Complete blood count red cell count
  3. C. Serum potassium level
  4. D. Serum lithium level
Answer: D. Serum lithium level

Q19. During a psychopharmacology teaching session, the nurse explains that antipsychotic medications can cause weight gain. Which of the following is an appropriate nursing strategy to help prevent or reduce this side effect?

  1. A. Encourage carbohydrate‑rich snacks between meals.
  2. B. Prescribe a daily dose of an appetite suppressant.
  3. C. Recommend a structured meal plan with regular physical activity.
  4. D. Advise the client to take the medication only at bedtime.
Answer: C. Recommend a structured meal plan with regular physical activity.

Q20. When a client shows signs of acute psychosis, which observation is the nurse’s highest priority?

  1. A. The client’s eye contact with others.
  2. B. The client’s ability to sniff the aroma of food.
  3. C. The client’s level of auditory hallucination.
  4. D. The client’s preference for dark rooms.
Answer: C. The client’s level of auditory hallucination.

Q21. Which nursing action represents the highest priority when initiating care for a client who has expressed active suicidal ideation?

  1. A. Assessing the client's home environment and outside social network
  2. B. Establishing a safe, closely monitored inpatient environment
  3. C. Discussing long-term educational and career goals
  4. D. Exploring the nurse's personal beliefs regarding depression
Answer: B. Establishing a safe, closely monitored inpatient environment

Q22. A client taking a newly prescribed psychiatric medication reports experiencing decreased appetite, mild headaches, and nausea. How should the nurse interpret these findings?

  1. A. Indications of a severe allergic anaphylactic reaction
  2. B. Common physical side effects frequently associated with psychotropic drug initiation
  3. C. Signs of complete treatment failure requiring immediate drug discontinuation
  4. D. Symptoms of acute tolerance and substance dependence
Answer: B. Common physical side effects frequently associated with psychotropic drug initiation

Q23. When providing education regarding psychopharmacology, what is a primary responsibility of the psychiatric nurse?

  1. A. Prescribing the correct dosage adjustments based on daily mood
  2. B. Understanding the mechanism of action and monitoring for expected and adverse effects
  3. C. Synthesizing new chemical compounds for faster symptom relief
  4. D. Diagnosing the underlying neurochemical imbalance independently
Answer: B. Understanding the mechanism of action and monitoring for expected and adverse effects

Q24. A client admitted for substance abuse treatment remarks to the nurse, "It felt so wonderful to get high." Which response by the nurse is most appropriate?

  1. A. "Why would you say something so self-destructive?"
  2. B. "Let's discuss what triggered those feelings and what led to that thought."
  3. C. "You should not dwell on past drug use if you want to recover."
  4. D. "It is normal to feel that way, so you don't need to worry about it."
Answer: B. "Let's discuss what triggered those feelings and what led to that thought."

Q25. During the management of a client experiencing acute alcohol withdrawal, which symptom requires the most urgent nursing vigilance?

  1. A. Mild daytime fatigue
  2. B. Transient skin dryness
  3. C. Severe autonomic hyperactivity and potential seizures
  4. D. Slightly decreased appetite
Answer: C. Severe autonomic hyperactivity and potential seizures
