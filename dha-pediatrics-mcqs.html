Q1. The first vaccine given at birth in most schedules includes:

  1. A. MMR
  2. B. BCG and Hepatitis B
  3. C. DPT booster
  4. D. HPV
Answer: B. BCG and Hepatitis B

Q2. A child of 1 year can usually:

  1. A. Run fast
  2. B. Walk with support and say a few words
  3. C. Read books
  4. D. Ride a bicycle
Answer: B. Walk with support and say a few words

Q3. The most common cause of diarrhoea in infants worldwide is:

  1. A. Bacteria
  2. B. Rotavirus
  3. C. Allergy
  4. D. Worms
Answer: B. Rotavirus

Q4. Oral rehydration solution (ORS) is the main treatment for:

  1. A. Pneumonia
  2. B. Dehydration from diarrhoea
  3. C. Fever
  4. D. Asthma
Answer: B. Dehydration from diarrhoea

Q5. A febrile seizure in a child is usually triggered by:

  1. A. Cold
  2. B. A rapid rise in body temperature
  3. C. Hunger
  4. D. Sleep
Answer: B. A rapid rise in body temperature

Q6. The normal heart rate of a newborn is about:

  1. A. 60-80 bpm
  2. B. 120-160 bpm
  3. C. 90-100 bpm
  4. D. 180-200 bpm
Answer: B. 120-160 bpm

Q7. Signs of respiratory distress in a child include:

  1. A. Slow breathing only
  2. B. Nasal flaring, chest retractions and grunting
  3. C. Crying loudly
  4. D. Playing actively
Answer: B. Nasal flaring, chest retractions and grunting

Q8. Exclusive breastfeeding is recommended for the first:

  1. A. 2 months
  2. B. 6 months
  3. C. 12 months
  4. D. 1 month
Answer: B. 6 months

Q9. Vitamin D deficiency in children causes:

  1. A. Scurvy
  2. B. Rickets
  3. C. Night blindness
  4. D. Anaemia
Answer: B. Rickets

Q10. A child with croup typically has:

  1. A. A barking cough and stridor
  2. B. A rash
  3. C. Diarrhoea
  4. D. Ear pain
Answer: A. A barking cough and stridor

Q11. Which of the following vaccines is routinely administered to a newborn infant at birth in many national immunization programs?

  1. A. Measles vaccine
  2. B. Bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine
  3. C. Varicella vaccine
  4. D. Meningococcal conjugate vaccine
Answer: B. Bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine

Q12. What is the recommended duration of exclusive breastfeeding according to World Health Organization guidelines?

  1. A. First 3 months of life
  2. B. First 6 months of life
  3. C. First 9 months of life
  4. D. First 12 months of life
Answer: B. First 6 months of life

Q13. Which developmental milestone is typically expected in a healthy full-term infant by 6 months of age?

  1. A. Walking independently
  2. B. Sitting unsupported
  3. C. Saying 3 to 5 words clearly
  4. D. Running and climbing stairs
Answer: B. Sitting unsupported

Q14. The most common causative agent of acute bronchiolitis in infants is:

  1. A. Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)
  2. B. Streptococcus pneumoniae
  3. C. Bordetella pertussis
  4. D. Influenza B virus
Answer: A. Respiratory syncytial virus (RSV)

Q15. A classic clinical feature of a child presenting with classic croup (laryngotracheobronchitis) is:

  1. A. Productive cough with blood-tinged sputum
  2. B. Barking cough and inspiratory stridor
  3. C. High fever and immediate drooling
  4. D. Silent chest with bilateral wheezing
Answer: B. Barking cough and inspiratory stridor

Q16. Vitamin D deficiency in young children primarily results in which of the following bone disorders?

  1. A. Osteogenesis imperfecta
  2. B. Rickets
  3. C. Scurvy
  4. D. Osteomyelitis
Answer: B. Rickets

Q17. Which of the following is the main indication for performing a lumbar puncture in a child with suspected encephalitis?

  1. A. Chest pain
  2. B. High fever with altered mental status
  3. C. Severe abdominal pain
  4. D. Persistent cough
Answer: B. High fever with altered mental status

Q18. In a 2‑year‑old child, which of the following findings is the most specific for an acute otitis media?

  1. A. Ringing in the ears
  2. B. Bulging, red tympanic membrane
  3. C. Dry cough
  4. D. Diarrhoea
Answer: B. Bulging, red tympanic membrane

Q19. A 3‑month‑old infant presents with persistent vomiting and lethargy. Which laboratory test is most useful to investigate a possible electrolyte imbalance?

  1. A. Serum magnesium
  2. B. Serum sodium
  3. C. Serum potassium
  4. D. Serum calcium
Answer: B. Serum sodium

Q20. Which respiratory sign is most commonly seen in a child with bronchiolitis?

  1. A. Tachycardia
  2. B. Wheezing
  3. C. Chest indrawing
  4. D. Pneumonia‑like crackles
Answer: C. Chest indrawing

Q21. During a routine well‑child visit, which of the following growth curves should the pediatrician reference for a 5‑year‑old boy?

  1. A. Adult male growth chart
  2. B. Child growth percentiles
  3. C. Teenage growth chart
  4. D. Infant growth chart
Answer: B. Child growth percentiles

Q22. A 9‑month‑old child has a history of sudden scratching and visible bites. The most likely etiologic agent is:

  1. A. Tinea capitis
  2. B. Scabies mite
  3. C. Ringworm
  4. D. Chickenpox virus
Answer: B. Scabies mite

Q23. Cushingoid features such as a rounded 'moon face' and truncal weight gain in a child are commonly associated with prolonged exposure to:

  1. A. Corticosteroids
  2. B. Growth hormone
  3. C. Thyroid hormone
  4. D. Insulin therapy
Answer: A. Corticosteroids

Q24. Which anatomical site is generally preferred for intramuscular injections in toddlers?

  1. A. Dorsogluteal muscle
  2. B. Vastus lateralis muscle
  3. C. Deltoid muscle
  4. D. Rectus abdominis muscle
Answer: B. Vastus lateralis muscle

Q25. A pediatric patient presenting with recurrent bone fractures, blue sclerae, and joint laxity is most suggestive of:

  1. A. Osteogenesis imperfecta
  2. B. Marfan syndrome
  3. C. Achondroplasia
  4. D. Rickets
Answer: A. Osteogenesis imperfecta
