Q1. What is the permissible limit of organic impurities in sand used for high-quality concrete work?

  1. A. Must be completely absent or within safe color standards
  2. B. Up to 5%
  3. C. Up to 10%
  4. D. Up to 15%
Answer: A. Must be completely absent or within safe color standards

Q2. What is the upper permissible limit for percentage of clay, silt, and fine dust combined in natural river sand as per standard specifications for structural concrete?

  1. A. Not more than 3 percent
  2. B. Not more than 8 percent
  3. C. Not more than 15 percent
  4. D. Not more than 20 percent
Answer: B. Not more than 8 percent

Q3. At approximately what percentage of moisture content by weight does sand typically exhibit its maximum possible volumetric bulking effect?

  1. A. A) 1% to 2%
  2. B. B) 5% to 8%
  3. C. C) 15% to 20%
  4. D. D) 25% to 30%
Answer: B. B) 5% to 8%

Q4. Which specific laboratory test procedure is primarily utilized to determine the fineness modulus and particle size distribution of a sand sample?

  1. A. A) Sedimentation test
  2. B. B) Sieve analysis test
  3. C. C) Specific gravity test
  4. D. D) Soundness test
Answer: B. B) Sieve analysis test

Q5. What is the primary physical consequence when fine sand contains an excessive amount of moisture, causing an apparent expansion in its dry volume?

  1. A. A) Bulking of sand
  2. B. B) Segregation of aggregate
  3. C. C) Efflorescence
  4. D. D) Bleeding
Answer: A. A) Bulking of sand

Q6. What is the maximum permissible percentage of silt and clay content in natural sand used for concrete work as per standard specifications?

  2. B. B) 5%
  3. C. C) 8%
  4. D. D) 10%
Answer: B. B) 5%

Q7. Which specific laboratory device is primarily utilized to determine the particle size distribution and fineness modulus of construction sand?

  4. D. D) Pycnometer

Q8. What is the term used to describe the volumetric increase of fine aggregate caused by the presence of surface moisture films separating the particles?

  1. A. Bulking of sand
  2. B. Segregation
  3. C. Bleeding
  4. D. Efflorescence
Answer: A. Bulking of sand

Q9. What is the primary physical consequence when fine sand contains an excessive amount of salt or saline contamination?

  1. A. A) Rapid hydration
  2. B. B) Efflorescence and reinforcement corrosion
  3. C. C) Increased compressive strength
  4. D. D) Elimination of shrinkage cracks

Q13. What term is used in concrete technology to describe the apparent volume increase of dry sand caused by the presence of surface moisture films?

  1. A. A) Segregation
  2. B. B) Bulking
  3. C. C) Bleeding
  4. D. D) Creep
Answer: B. B) Bulking

Q14. According to standard classification based on particle size distribution, sand having a fineness modulus falling between 2.2 and 2.6 is generally designated as:

  1. A. A) Fine sand
  2. B. B) Medium sand
  3. C. C) Coarse sand
  4. D. D) Very fine sand
Answer: B. B) Medium sand

Q15. What is the primary effect of excessive silt content exceeding standard limits in fine aggregate used for reinforced cement concrete?

  1. A. Increases bonding strength with steel
  2. B. Reduces drying shrinkage
  3. C. Decreases compressive strength and increases water demand
  4. D. Accelerates the initial setting time of cement
Answer: C. Decreases compressive strength and increases water demand

Q18. Bulking of sand refers to which of the following phenomena?

  1. A. A) Increase in volume due to presence of moisture
  2. B. B) Increase in weight due to high temperature
  3. C. C) Reduction in volume due to compaction
  4. D. D) Swelling of clay particles in dry air
Answer: A. A) Increase in volume due to presence of moisture
