Q1. Variable cost per unit is generally:

  1. A. Constant
  2. B. Zero
  3. C. Equal to fixed cost
  4. D. Rising with volume
Answer: A. Constant

Q2. Break-even sales in units equal fixed costs divided by:

  1. A. Profit per unit
  2. B. Selling price
  3. C. Variable cost
  4. D. Contribution per unit
Answer: D. Contribution per unit

Q3. Margin of safety is the difference between actual sales and:

  1. A. Break-even sales
  2. B. Variable costs
  3. C. Budgeted costs
  4. D. Fixed costs
Answer: A. Break-even sales

Q4. A flexible budget is adjusted for:

  1. A. Inflation only
  2. B. Tax rates
  3. C. Last year's costs
  4. D. Actual activity level
Answer: D. Actual activity level

Q5. Job costing is suitable where products are:

  1. A. Sold from stock only
  2. B. Made to customer specification
  3. C. Purchased for resale
  4. D. Identical and continuous
Answer: B. Made to customer specification

Q6. Process costing is suitable for:

  1. A. One-off projects
  2. B. Consulting engagements
  3. C. Construction contracts
  4. D. Continuous mass production
Answer: D. Continuous mass production

Q7. An adverse material price variance means actual price was:

  1. A. Lower than standard
  2. B. Unrelated to standard
  3. C. Equal to standard
  4. D. Higher than standard
Answer: D. Higher than standard

Q8. In decision making, the benefit given up by choosing one alternative over the next best is called:

  1. A. Standard cost
  2. B. Fixed cost
  3. C. Sunk cost
  4. D. Opportunity cost
Answer: D. Opportunity cost

Q9. Overheads are usually apportioned using:

  1. A. An appropriate basis such as floor area
  2. B. Customer count only
  3. C. Selling price only
  4. D. Random numbers
Answer: A. An appropriate basis such as floor area

Q10. The relevant cost of a decision includes only costs that are:

  1. A. Future and differ between alternatives
  2. B. Allocated arbitrarily
  3. C. Sunk
  4. D. Historic
Answer: A. Future and differ between alternatives

Q11. Which pricing method sets a very high initial price for a novel product to skim maximum profits before competitors enter?

  1. A. Penetration pricing
  2. B. Skimming pricing
  3. C. Cost-plus pricing
  4. D. Marginal cost pricing
Answer: B. Skimming pricing

Q12. In standard costing, what type of variance occurs when actual material usage exceeds the standard quantity allowed for actual production?

  1. A. Favorable material usage variance
  2. B. Adverse material usage variance
  3. C. Favorable labor efficiency variance
  4. D. Adverse overhead volume variance
Answer: B. Adverse material usage variance

Q13. What is calculated by dividing fixed manufacturing overheads by the budgeted production volume in standard costing?

  1. A. Variable overhead rate
  2. B. Predetermined fixed overhead absorption rate
  3. C. Contribution margin ratio
  4. D. Margin of safety percentage
Answer: B. Predetermined fixed overhead absorption rate

Q14. Which budget serves as the central plan summarizing all functional budgets before final management approval?

  1. A. Cash budget
  2. B. Master budget
  3. C. Flexible budget
  4. D. Capital expenditure budget
Answer: B. Master budget

Q15. What term describes a cost that has already been incurred and cannot be altered by any current or future decision?

  1. A. Opportunity cost
  2. B. Relevant cost
  3. C. Sunk cost
  4. D. Incremental cost
Answer: C. Sunk cost

Q16. Under throughput accounting, which cost is generally treated as the only truly relevant variable cost when calculating throughput contribution?

  1. A. Direct labor cost
  2. B. Direct material cost
  3. C. Factory rent
  4. D. Depreciation of machinery
Answer: B. Direct material cost

Q17. What is the primary orientation of management accounting information compared to financial accounting?

  1. A. Strictly historical and mandatory
  2. B. Focused mainly on future planning and control
  3. C. Exclusively targeted for external tax authorities
  4. D. Restricted to statutory annual reporting
Answer: B. Focused mainly on future planning and control

Q18. Under absorption costing, what situation occurs when the absorbed manufacturing overheads are less than the actual overheads incurred during the period?

  1. A. Over-absorption of overheads
  2. B. Complete absorption of overheads
  3. C. Under-absorption of overheads
  4. D. Zero overhead variance
Answer: C. Under-absorption of overheads

Q19. When designing management accounting systems, what guiding principle determines the choice of internal methods used by a firm?

  1. A. Strict adherence to external regulatory templates
  2. B. Using whatever methods the company finds beneficial
  3. C. Mandatory compliance with statutory tax codes
  4. D. Standardized formats prescribed by financial auditors
Answer: B. Using whatever methods the company finds beneficial

Q20. If the closing inventory of finished goods increases compared to the opening inventory, how do profits under absorption costing generally compare to marginal costing?

  1. A. Profits under absorption costing will be higher
  2. B. Profits under absorption costing will be lower
  3. C. Profits under both methods will remain identical
  4. D. Absorption costing profit cannot be determined
Answer: A. Profits under absorption costing will be higher

Q21. What term describes the condition when actual manufacturing overhead costs are lower than the total overheads absorbed into production?

  1. A. Under-absorbed overheads
  2. B. Over-absorbed overheads
  3. C. Sunk overheads
  4. D. Discretionary overheads
Answer: B. Over-absorbed overheads

Q22. Which of the following functions represents a core internal application of management accounting rather than external reporting?

  1. A. Preparing final statutory accounts for shareholders
  2. B. Filing annual corporate tax returns
  3. C. Internal decision making and cost control
  4. D. Auditing historical balance sheet items
Answer: C. Internal decision making and cost control

Q23. Which of the following describes the primary orientation of management accounting information compared to financial accounting?

  1. A. Focused heavily on future planning and internal decision-making
  2. B. Restricted strictly to historical external reporting rules
  3. C. Mandated by statutory corporate legislation for shareholders
  4. D. Prepared exclusively for external tax authorities and banks
Answer: A. Focused heavily on future planning and internal decision-making

Q24. What is the primary guiding principle when a company designs its internal management accounting information system?

  1. A. Using whatever methods the company finds beneficial and relevant
  2. B. Strictly following mandatory tax legislation and legal formats
  3. C. Ensuring all reports are standardized for public stock exchanges
  4. D. Eliminating all variances through strict historical compliance
Answer: A. Using whatever methods the company finds beneficial and relevant

Q25. Which of the following options represents a core internal function served by management accounting?

  1. A. Assisting managers in internal decision-making and planning
  2. B. Preparing final statutory accounts for publication
  3. C. Submitting corporate annual tax returns to government agencies
  4. D. Raising long-term public debt and equity capital
Answer: A. Assisting managers in internal decision-making and planning
