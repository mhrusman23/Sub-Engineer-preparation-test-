Q1. The external auditor expresses an opinion on whether financial statements give a:

  1. A. Perfect forecast
  2. B. Complete list of customers
  3. C. Guaranteed profit
  4. D. True and fair view
Answer: D. True and fair view

Q2. Audit risk is made up of inherent risk, control risk and:

  1. A. Detection risk
  2. B. Liquidity risk
  3. C. Reputation risk
  4. D. Business risk only
Answer: A. Detection risk

Q3. Information is material if its omission or misstatement could influence:

  1. A. Only the directors
  2. B. Only the auditor
  3. C. Economic decisions of users
  4. D. Only tax authorities
Answer: C. Economic decisions of users

Q4. Tests of controls are designed to assess the:

  1. A. Going concern only
  2. B. Existence of cash only
  3. C. Operating effectiveness of controls
  4. D. Completeness of equity
Answer: C. Operating effectiveness of controls

Q5. Observing the inventory count mainly provides evidence of:

  1. A. Rights and obligations
  2. B. Valuation of receivables
  3. C. Existence
  4. D. Presentation
Answer: C. Existence

Q6. Which is a threat to auditor independence?

  1. A. Rotating audit staff
  2. B. Using a checklist
  3. C. Self-interest
  4. D. Peer review
Answer: C. Self-interest

Q7. A modified audit opinion can be qualified, adverse or:

  1. A. Disclaimer of opinion
  2. B. Emphasised
  3. C. Clean
  4. D. Unqualified
Answer: A. Disclaimer of opinion

Q8. Evidence from third parties is generally more reliable than evidence:

  1. A. From the auditor
  2. B. From an independent bank
  3. C. From a regulator
  4. D. Generated internally by the client
Answer: D. Generated internally by the client

Q9. A written confirmation from management about key matters is a:

  1. A. Prospectus
  2. B. Engagement letter only
  3. C. Letter of representation
  4. D. Management report
Answer: C. Letter of representation

Q10. Substantive procedures include tests of details and:

  1. A. Analytical procedures
  2. B. Ethics checks
  3. C. Control walkthroughs only
  4. D. Board minutes only
Answer: A. Analytical procedures

Q11. What is the primary objective of performing analytical procedures during the overall review stage of an audit?

  1. A. To test internal controls
  2. B. To assist in forming an overall conclusion on whether the financial statements are consistent with the auditor's understanding
  3. C. To calculate the exact sample size for substantive testing
  4. D. To detect all instances of employee fraud
Answer: B. To assist in forming an overall conclusion on whether the financial statements are consistent with the auditor's understanding

Q12. How does the implementation of an Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) system typically affect internal controls within an organization?

  1. A. It eliminates the need for any management oversight
  2. B. It centralizes data and strengthens automated application controls
  3. C. It completely removes the risk of management override
  4. D. It makes physical inventory counts unnecessary
Answer: B. It centralizes data and strengthens automated application controls

Q13. Which of the following is an example of an internal control activity within the COSO framework?

  1. A. Management philosophy and operating style
  2. B. Segregation of duties
  3. C. The integrity and ethical values of personnel
  4. D. The board of directors' oversight responsibility
Answer: B. Segregation of duties

Q14. Which of the following is considered a primary control activity in an audit engagement?

  1. A. Market research
  2. B. Segregation of duties
  3. C. Product development
  4. D. Sales forecasting
Answer: B. Segregation of duties

Q15. What is the primary objective of financial statement auditing?

  1. A. To detect all instances of fraud and error
  2. B. To prepare the financial statements on behalf of management
  3. C. To express an opinion on whether the financial statements are prepared in all material respects in accordance with an applicable financial reporting framework
  4. D. To guarantee the absolute future profitability of the entity
Answer: C. To express an opinion on whether the financial statements are prepared in all material respects in accordance with an applicable financial reporting framework

Q16. In auditing, what is the primary purpose of substantive procedures?

  1. A. To test the operating effectiveness of internal controls
  2. B. To detect material misstatements at the assertion level
  3. C. To calculate the total tax liability of the client
  4. D. To evaluate the corporate governance structure
Answer: B. To detect material misstatements at the assertion level

Q17. What is the primary objective of the auditor when conducting an audit under ISA 315?

  1. A. To express an opinion on the effectiveness of internal control
  2. B. To identify and assess the risks of material misstatement
  3. C. To detect all errors and frauds in the financial statements
  4. D. To prepare the financial statements on behalf of management
Answer: B. To identify and assess the risks of material misstatement

Q18. In the context of corporate governance, what is the primary role of the internal audit function?

  1. A. To prepare the annual financial statements
  2. B. To provide independent and objective assurance on risk management and internal controls
  3. C. To set executive remuneration policies
  4. D. To audit the external auditors' working papers
Answer: B. To provide independent and objective assurance on risk management and internal controls

Q19. What is the primary objective of performing a substantive analytical procedure during an external audit?

  1. A. To test the operating effectiveness of internal controls
  2. B. To detect material misstatements at the assertion level
  3. C. To assess the competence of management
  4. D. To determine the sample size for physical inventory count
Answer: B. To detect material misstatements at the assertion level

Q20. Which audit procedure is most effective in testing the completeness assertion for trade payables?

  1. A. Tracing a sample of recorded purchase invoices to suppliers' statements
  2. B. Vouching a sample of recorded trade payables to underlying goods received notes and supplier invoices
  3. C. Sending confirmation letters to customers with large credit balances
  4. D. Testing post-year-end cash disbursements and checking if the related liability was recorded in the correct period
Answer: D. Testing post-year-end cash disbursements and checking if the related liability was recorded in the correct period

Q21. What type of audit opinion is issued when the financial statements are materially misstated and the effects are both material and pervasive?

  1. A. Unqualified opinion
  2. B. Qualified opinion
  3. C. Adverse opinion
  4. D. Disclaimer of opinion
Answer: C. Adverse opinion

Q22. When performing analytical procedures during the planning phase of an audit, what is the primary purpose?

  1. A. To gather direct evidence for specific account balances
  2. B. To identify unusual transactions, events, and trends that might indicate material misstatement risks
  3. C. To replace tests of details entirely
  4. D. To calculate the final audit opinion
Answer: B. To identify unusual transactions, events, and trends that might indicate material misstatement risks

Q23. When assessing going concern, for what minimum period from the date of approval of financial statements must management assess the entity's ability to continue?

  1. A. At least 6 months
  2. B. Exactly 12 months
  3. C. At least 12 months from the reporting period end or approval date
  4. D. Indefinitely into the future
Answer: C. At least 12 months from the reporting period end or approval date

Q24. Which type of control test aims directly to verify whether authorization policies for major capital expenditures are operating effectively?

  1. A. Substantive analytical procedure
  2. B. Test of controls
  3. C. External confirmation
  4. D. Substantive test of details
Answer: B. Test of controls

Q25. What is the primary objective of sending accounts receivable confirmations directly to debtors during an audit?

  1. A. To test the existence of recorded receivables
  2. B. To evaluate the client's credit control policy
  3. C. To calculate the total bad debt provision
  4. D. To verify the accuracy of inventory valuation
Answer: A. To test the existence of recorded receivables
