Q1. The most common cause of post-operative fever in the first 24 hours is:

  1. A. Wound infection
  2. B. Atelectasis (collapsed lung areas)
  3. C. Blood clot
  4. D. Allergy
Answer: B. Atelectasis (collapsed lung areas)

Q2. Before surgery, patients are kept fasting (NPO) mainly to prevent:

  1. A. Hunger
  2. B. Aspiration of stomach contents during anaesthesia
  3. C. Infection
  4. D. Bleeding
Answer: B. Aspiration of stomach contents during anaesthesia

Q3. A surgical site infection usually appears:

  1. A. Immediately
  2. B. Around 3-7 days after surgery
  3. C. After one year
  4. D. Before surgery
Answer: B. Around 3-7 days after surgery

Q4. The classic signs of appendicitis include pain starting around the umbilicus and moving to:

  1. A. The left side
  2. B. The right iliac fossa (McBurney's point)
  3. C. The shoulder
  4. D. The back
Answer: B. The right iliac fossa (McBurney's point)

Q5. Informed consent for surgery must be obtained:

  1. A. After surgery
  2. B. Before surgery, from the patient or legal guardian
  3. C. From the nurse
  4. D. It is not needed
Answer: B. Before surgery, from the patient or legal guardian

Q6. A patient with intestinal obstruction typically presents with:

  1. A. Vomiting, distension and absolute constipation
  2. B. Diarrhoea only
  3. C. Fever only
  4. D. Rash
Answer: A. Vomiting, distension and absolute constipation

Q7. The universal protocol before surgery includes verifying:

  1. A. The bill
  2. B. Correct patient, site and procedure (time-out)
  3. C. The weather
  4. D. Visiting hours
Answer: B. Correct patient, site and procedure (time-out)

Q8. Post-operative bleeding is most likely detected by:

  1. A. Tachycardia and falling blood pressure
  2. B. High temperature
  3. C. Hunger
  4. D. Itching
Answer: A. Tachycardia and falling blood pressure

Q9. Gallstones most commonly cause pain in the:

  1. A. Left lower abdomen
  2. B. Right upper abdomen
  3. C. Chest
  4. D. Groin
Answer: B. Right upper abdomen

Q10. A hernia that becomes painful, tense and irreducible is:

  1. A. Improving
  2. B. Strangulated — a surgical emergency
  3. C. Normal
  4. D. A muscle strain
Answer: B. Strangulated — a surgical emergency

Q11. What is the primary rationale for keeping surgical patients fasting (nil per os or NPO) prior to receiving general anesthesia?

  1. A. To reduce intraoperative blood loss
  2. B. To prevent the aspiration of gastric contents into the respiratory tract
  3. C. To accelerate the metabolic clearance of intravenous medications
  4. D. To completely eliminate normal bowel sounds during surgery
Answer: B. To prevent the aspiration of gastric contents into the respiratory tract

Q12. Which specific anatomical point is most classically associated with maximum tenderness during physical examination for acute appendicitis?

  1. A. Murphy's point
  2. B. McBurney's point
  3. C. Erb's point
  4. D. Lanz's point
Answer: B. McBurney's point

Q13. How is a surgical hernia classified when it becomes acutely painful, tense, and its contents can no longer be pushed back into the abdominal cavity?

  1. A. Reducible and benign
  2. B. Strangulated and requiring immediate surgical intervention
  3. C. Incisional and chronic
  4. D. Congenital and asymptomatic
Answer: B. Strangulated and requiring immediate surgical intervention

Q14. Which clinical combination of vital sign changes serves as the earliest reliable indicator of acute internal hemorrhage following a major surgical procedure?

  1. A. Bradycardia and soaring blood pressure
  2. B. Tachycardia and falling systemic blood pressure
  3. C. Hyperthermia and bradypnea
  4. D. Normal heart rate with elevated oxygen saturation
Answer: B. Tachycardia and falling systemic blood pressure

Q15. What is the mandatory final step immediately prior to making a surgical incision, designed to prevent wrong-site or wrong-patient errors?

  1. A. Administering a prophylactic antacid
  2. B. Performing the surgical time-out procedure
  3. C. Ordering a baseline post-operative complete blood count
  4. D. Securing the patient's written financial consent
Answer: B. Performing the surgical time-out procedure

Q16. In the management of suspected acute cholecystitis, localized right upper quadrant abdominal pain is most frequently precipitated by:

  1. A. Renal calculi moving down the ureter
  2. B. Gallstones obstructing the cystic duct
  3. C. Inflammation of the lower colonic diverticula
  4. D. Gastric ulcer perforation
Answer: B. Gallstones obstructing the cystic duct

Q17. Which specific surgical instrument is primarily utilized for clamping blood vessels to achieve hemostasis during dissection?

  1. A. Metzenbaum scissors
  2. B. Crile forceps
  3. C. Semb retractor
  4. D. Adson forceps
Answer: B. Crile forceps

Q18. What is the primary physiological consequence of untreated tension pneumothorax during intraoperative management?

  1. A. Metabolic alkalosis
  2. B. Increased venous return
  3. C. Obstructive shock
  4. D. Peripheral vasodilation
Answer: C. Obstructive shock

Q19. Which fluid resuscitation formula is traditionally applied in the initial management of severe thermal burns within the first 24 hours?

  1. A. Parkland formula
  2. B. Malignant hyperthermia protocol
  3. C. Glasgow scoring model
  4. D. Apache II index
Answer: A. Parkland formula

Q20. What is the standard diagnostic imaging modality of choice for confirming acute appendicitis in pediatric and pregnant surgical patients?

  1. A. Abdominal plain radiograph
  2. B. Ultrasonography
  3. C. Contrast-enhanced CT scan
  4. D. Magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography
Answer: B. Ultrasonography

Q21. Which specific classification scale is universally utilized to grade the physical status of surgical patients prior to anesthesia administration?

  1. A. ASA physical status classification
  2. B. Balthazar severity index
  3. C. Child-Pugh score
  4. D. Ranson criteria
Answer: A. ASA physical status classification

Q22. What is the primary characteristic feature distinguishing a sliding hiatal hernia from a paraesophageal hernia?

  1. A. Displacement of the gastroesophageal junction into the thorax
  2. B. Complete rotation of the gastric body around its axis
  3. C. Necrosis of the small bowel loops
  4. D. Elevation of the right hemidiaphragm exclusively
Answer: A. Displacement of the gastroesophageal junction into the thorax

Q23. Which of the following electrolyte imbalances is characterized by a serum sodium level below 135 mEq/L and requires careful monitoring during fluid management?

  1. A. Hyponatremia
  2. B. Hyperkalemia
  3. C. Hypercalcemia
  4. D. Hypomagnesemia
Answer: A. Hyponatremia

Q24. Which bacterial pathogen is well known for producing a potent toxin that causes massive fluid secretion into the intestinal lumen, leading to severe watery diarrhea?

  1. A. Salmonella typhi
  2. B. Shigella dysenteriae
  3. C. Vibrio cholerae
  4. D. Escherichia coli
Answer: C. Vibrio cholerae

Q25. Which type of shock is primarily managed through aggressive fluid resuscitation alongside addressing the underlying cause of acute volume loss?

  1. A. Hypovolemic shock
  2. B. Neurogenic shock
  3. C. Anaphylactic shock
  4. D. Septic shock
Answer: A. Hypovolemic shock
