Q1. Which material is commonly used as a good conductor in electrical wiring?

  1. A. Copper
  2. B. Rubber
  3. C. Glass
  4. D. Plastic
Answer: A. Copper

Q2. A fuse wire should have:

  1. A. High melting point and high resistance
  2. B. Low melting point and high resistance
  3. C. High melting point and low resistance
  4. D. Low melting point and low resistance
Answer: B. Low melting point and high resistance

Q3. Which safety device is used to protect a circuit from excess current?

  1. A. Transformer
  2. B. Fuse
  3. C. Diode
  4. D. Resistor
Answer: B. Fuse

Q4. The rating of a fuse wire is expressed in terms of:

  1. A. Current
  2. B. Voltage
  3. C. Resistance
  4. D. Power
Answer: A. Current

Q5. The colour of earth wire in modern wiring is commonly:

  1. A. Red
  2. B. Blue
  3. C. Black
  4. D. Green or green-yellow
Answer: D. Green or green-yellow

Q6. A fuse or circuit breaker is rated in:

  1. A. Henries
  2. B. Amperes
  3. C. Volts
  4. D. Ohms
Answer: B. Amperes

Q7. Earthing is done to protect against:

  1. A. Low voltage
  2. B. Electric shock
  3. C. Low frequency
  4. D. Long wires
Answer: B. Electric shock

Q8. An RCD or GFCI trips when it detects:

  1. A. High resistance
  2. B. Low voltage
  3. C. Leakage current
  4. D. High frequency
Answer: C. Leakage current

Q9. Wet hands while using electrical equipment increase the risk of:

  1. A. Fuse gain
  2. B. Heat gain
  3. C. Electric shock
  4. D. Power gain
Answer: C. Electric shock

Q10. Which is a good conductor of electricity?

  1. A. Plastic
  2. B. Rubber
  3. C. Glass
  4. D. Copper
Answer: D. Copper

Q11. PVC is used on wires as:

  1. A. Switch
  2. B. Insulation
  3. C. Fuse
  4. D. Conductor
Answer: B. Insulation

Q12. Overloading a circuit can cause:

  1. A. Lower bills
  2. B. Overheating and fire
  3. C. Higher voltage
  4. D. Higher speed
Answer: B. Overheating and fire

Q13. Before working on a circuit, you should first:

  1. A. Add load
  2. B. Use wet tools
  3. C. Switch off and isolate the supply
  4. D. Touch the wires
Answer: C. Switch off and isolate the supply

Q14. A switch should always be placed in the:

  1. A. Earth wire
  2. B. Neutral wire
  3. C. Live wire
  4. D. Any wire
Answer: C. Live wire

Q15. What is the primary purpose of a residual current device (RCD) in household wiring?

  1. A. To limit current flow during overload
  2. B. To detect and disconnect leakage currents to earth
  3. C. To increase the voltage supplied to appliances
  4. D. To provide a backup power source
Answer: B. To detect and disconnect leakage currents to earth

Q16. What is the main reason for using a fuse with a low melting point in a circuit?

  1. A. To allow the fuse to conduct more current
  2. B. To ensure the fuse melts quickly during overcurrent
  3. C. To increase the resistance of the circuit
  4. D. To reduce the cost of the fuse
Answer: B. To ensure the fuse melts quickly during overcurrent

Q17. When installing a new light fixture, where should the switch be placed for best safety practice?

  1. A. Between the light socket and the power supply
  2. B. Between the power supply and the light socket
  3. C. At the end of the light fixture
  4. D. It does not matter where the switch is placed
Answer: B. Between the power supply and the light socket

Q18. Which colour is typically used for the neutral conductor in modern residential wiring?

  1. A. Blue
  2. B. Red
  3. C. Green
  4. D. Yellow
Answer: A. Blue

Q19. Which of the following is NOT a typical function of an earthing system?

  1. A. Providing a low‑resistance path for fault currents
  2. B. Preventing electric shock by grounding stray currents
  3. C. Increasing the voltage level of the supply
  4. D. Stabilising the system voltage during faults
Answer: C. Increasing the voltage level of the supply

Q20. Which of the following practices is considered acceptable under standard workplace safety regulations?

  1. A. Removing a ground pin to fit a three-prong plug into a two-slot receptacle
  2. B. Using flexible cords as a permanent substitute for fixed building wiring
  3. C. Allowing a trained and qualified person to repair a damaged extension cord
  4. D. Daisy-chaining multiple extension cords together to reach distant equipment
Answer: C. Allowing a trained and qualified person to repair a damaged extension cord

Q21. What is the term used when maintenance or repair tasks are performed directly on or within reaching distance of energized electrical parts?

  1. A. De-energized work
  2. B. Isolated work
  3. C. Energized work
  4. D. Passive testing
Answer: C. Energized work

Q22. What is the primary hazard associated with open knockouts left empty on electrical junction or outlet boxes?

  1. A. Increased circuit resistance
  2. B. Unintended access to live wires posing shock and fire risks
  3. C. Excessive voltage drop across the terminal blocks
  4. D. Premature tripping of downstream miniature circuit breakers
Answer: B. Unintended access to live wires posing shock and fire risks

Q23. Which electrical hazard involves a sudden, high-power release of energy through the air, producing blinding light and intense heat?

  1. A. Dielectric breakdown
  2. B. Arc flash
  3. C. Corona discharge
  4. D. Inductive kickback
Answer: B. Arc flash

Q24. What term describes an electrical conductor that lacks proper insulation or physical protection barriers?

  1. A. Exposed
  2. B. Grounded
  3. C. Shielded
  4. D. Isolated
Answer: A. Exposed

Q25. Which physical measurement describes the rate of flow of electric charge passing a given point in a circuit per second?

  1. A. Voltage
  2. B. Current
  3. C. Resistance
  4. D. Capacitance
Answer: B. Current
