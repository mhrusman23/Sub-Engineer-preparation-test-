Q1. The father of modern Public Administration as a discipline is considered:

  1. A. Max Weber
  2. B. Woodrow Wilson
  3. C. Herbert Simon
  4. D. F.W. Taylor
Answer: B. Woodrow Wilson

Q2. Bureaucracy, according to Weber, is characterised by:

  1. A. Personal favours
  2. B. Rules, hierarchy and written records
  3. C. No structure
  4. D. Hereditary posts
Answer: B. Rules, hierarchy and written records

Q3. POSDCORB — the functions of management — was given by:

  1. A. Fayol
  2. B. Luther Gulick
  3. C. Maslow
  4. D. Herzberg
Answer: B. Luther Gulick

Q4. New Public Management (NPM) emphasises:

  1. A. More rules
  2. B. Market-like practices, efficiency and results in government
  3. C. Bigger offices
  4. D. Lifetime jobs only
Answer: B. Market-like practices, efficiency and results in government

Q5. Accountability in administration means officials must:

  1. A. Hide decisions
  2. B. Answer for their actions and use of public resources
  3. C. Avoid audits
  4. D. Work independently of law
Answer: B. Answer for their actions and use of public resources

Q6. Decentralisation means:

  1. A. All power at the centre
  2. B. Transfer of authority to lower/local levels
  3. C. Closing local offices
  4. D. Privatisation
Answer: B. Transfer of authority to lower/local levels

Q7. Maslow's hierarchy of needs starts with:

  1. A. Self-actualisation
  2. B. Physiological needs
  3. C. Esteem
  4. D. Safety
Answer: B. Physiological needs

Q8. An ombudsman is an official who:

  1. A. Collects taxes
  2. B. Investigates citizens' complaints against administration
  3. C. Commands the police
  4. D. Writes laws
Answer: B. Investigates citizens' complaints against administration

Q9. E-governance uses technology to:

  1. A. Reduce services
  2. B. Deliver public services more efficiently and transparently
  3. C. Replace all officials
  4. D. Increase paperwork
Answer: B. Deliver public services more efficiently and transparently

Q10. Span of control refers to:

  1. A. Office size
  2. B. The number of subordinates one manager can effectively supervise
  3. C. Budget size
  4. D. Working hours
Answer: B. The number of subordinates one manager can effectively supervise

Q11. In the study of public administration, the implementation of administrative policies directly contributes to the maintenance of:

  1. A. Stock market liquidity
  2. B. Bureaucratic governance structures
  3. C. Monarchical succession laws
  4. D. Private retail monopolies
Answer: B. Bureaucratic governance structures

Q12. Which managerial approach heavily inspired the results-oriented and efficiency-driven reforms seen in New Public Management?

  1. A. Taylor's Scientific Management
  2. B. Feudal governance structures
  3. C. Guild socialism
  4. D. Patronage system
Answer: A. Taylor's Scientific Management

Q13. Which administrative model primarily relies on private sector financing and collaboration for delivering public infrastructure and services?

  1. A. Traditional Public Administration
  2. B. Public-Private Partnership (PPP)
  3. C. New Public Management
  4. D. Bureaucratic Centralization
Answer: B. Public-Private Partnership (PPP)

Q14. What core characteristic distinguishes the Public-Private Partnership (PPP) framework from traditional government procurement?

  1. A. Complete elimination of government oversight
  2. B. Reliance on private sector funding for public asset creation
  3. C. Exclusive reliance on international grants
  4. D. Prohibition of private sector involvement
Answer: B. Reliance on private sector funding for public asset creation

Q15. New Public Management (NPM) is often viewed conceptually as a modern derivative and administrative variant of:

  1. A. Max Weber’s Ideal Type of Bureaucracy
  2. B. Frederick W. Taylor’s Scientific Management
  3. C. Karl Marx’s Conflict Theory
  4. D. Emile Durkheim’s Structural Functionalism
Answer: B. Frederick W. Taylor’s Scientific Management

Q16. The primary focus of public administration within governmental frameworks is largely concerned with executing and upholding:

  1. A. Bureaucratic governance mechanisms
  2. B. Private corporate strategies
  3. C. Purely judicial verdicts
  4. D. Legislative election campaigns
Answer: A. Bureaucratic governance mechanisms

Q17. An independent public official appointed to investigate citizen complaints against government agencies is known as an:

  1. A. Auditor General
  2. B. Ombudsman
  3. C. Magistrate
  4. D. Arbitrator
Answer: B. Ombudsman

Q18. The administrative framework where officials follow a strict hierarchy, clear rules, and formalized procedures is called:

  1. A. Patronage Bureaucracy
  2. B. Ideal Type of Bureaucracy
  3. C. Charismatic Administration
  4. D. Adhocracy
Answer: B. Ideal Type of Bureaucracy

Q19. The acronym POSDCORB, outlining fundamental executive functions, was originally introduced by:

  1. A. Luther Gulick
  2. B. Max Weber
  3. C. Woodrow Wilson
  4. D. Herbert Simon
Answer: A. Luther Gulick

Q20. The administrative practice of transferring decision-making authority from central headquarters to regional or field units is called:

  1. A. Centralization
  2. B. Decentralization
  3. C. Privatization
  4. D. Nationalization
Answer: B. Decentralization

Q21. The foundational separation between policy formulation and policy execution is best known as:

  1. A. The Bureaucratic-Managerial Split
  2. B. The Politics-Administration Dichotomy
  3. C. The Executive-Legislative Division
  4. D. The State-Market Dichotomy
Answer: B. The Politics-Administration Dichotomy

Q22. In Maslow's motivational hierarchy, the foundational tier that must be satisfied first consists of:

  1. A. Social affiliation needs
  2. B. Physiological needs
  3. C. Self-actualization needs
  4. D. Esteem needs
Answer: B. Physiological needs

Q23. Decision-making by public administrators within the flexible boundaries established by law is best described as:

  1. A. A) Legislative fiat
  2. B. B) Administrative discretion
  3. C. C) Judicial activism
  4. D. D) Executive override
Answer: B. B) Administrative discretion

Q24. Adherence to established ethical standards and moral uprightness in official duties is referred to as:

  1. A. A) Administrative morality
  2. B. B) Bureaucratic efficiency
  3. C. C) Public accountability
  4. D. D) Strategic planning
Answer: A. A) Administrative morality

Q25. The core administrative principle which ensures that all individuals and institutions are accountable to publicly promulgated laws is known as:

  1. A. A) Executive privilege
  2. B. B) Rule of law
  3. C. C) Ministerial responsibility
  4. D. D) Administrative immunity
Answer: B. B) Rule of law
