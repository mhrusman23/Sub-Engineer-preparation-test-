Q1. The father of modern Public Administration as a discipline is considered:

  1. A. Max Weber
  2. B. Woodrow Wilson
  3. C. Herbert Simon
  4. D. F.W. Taylor
Answer: B. Woodrow Wilson

Q2. Bureaucracy, according to Weber, is characterised by:

  1. A. Personal favours
  2. B. Rules, hierarchy and written records
  3. C. No structure
  4. D. Hereditary posts
Answer: B. Rules, hierarchy and written records

Q3. POSDCORB — the functions of management — was given by:

  1. A. Fayol
  2. B. Luther Gulick
  3. C. Maslow
  4. D. Herzberg
Answer: B. Luther Gulick

Q4. New Public Management (NPM) emphasises:

  1. A. More rules
  2. B. Market-like practices, efficiency and results in government
  3. C. Bigger offices
  4. D. Lifetime jobs only
Answer: B. Market-like practices, efficiency and results in government

Q5. Accountability in administration means officials must:

  1. A. Hide decisions
  2. B. Answer for their actions and use of public resources
  3. C. Avoid audits
  4. D. Work independently of law
Answer: B. Answer for their actions and use of public resources

Q6. Decentralisation means:

  1. A. All power at the centre
  2. B. Transfer of authority to lower/local levels
  3. C. Closing local offices
  4. D. Privatisation
Answer: B. Transfer of authority to lower/local levels

Q7. Maslow's hierarchy of needs starts with:

  1. A. Self-actualisation
  2. B. Physiological needs
  3. C. Esteem
  4. D. Safety
Answer: B. Physiological needs

Q8. An ombudsman is an official who:

  1. A. Collects taxes
  2. B. Investigates citizens' complaints against administration
  3. C. Commands the police
  4. D. Writes laws
Answer: B. Investigates citizens' complaints against administration

Q9. E-governance uses technology to:

  1. A. Reduce services
  2. B. Deliver public services more efficiently and transparently
  3. C. Replace all officials
  4. D. Increase paperwork
Answer: B. Deliver public services more efficiently and transparently

Q10. Span of control refers to:

  1. A. Office size
  2. B. The number of subordinates one manager can effectively supervise
  3. C. Budget size
  4. D. Working hours
Answer: B. The number of subordinates one manager can effectively supervise

Q11. Woodrow Wilson's essay 'The Study of Administration', which launched the discipline, was published in:

  1. A. 1887
  2. B. 1900
  3. C. 1926
  4. D. 1937
Answer: A. 1887

Q12. Woodrow Wilson is best known in administrative theory for advocating:

  1. A. the spoils system
  2. B. the separation of politics from administration
  3. C. scientific management
  4. D. the human relations approach
Answer: B. the separation of politics from administration

Q13. The book 'Politics and Administration' (1900) was written by:

  1. A. Dwight Waldo
  2. B. Herbert Simon
  3. C. Frank J. Goodnow
  4. D. Leonard D. White
Answer: C. Frank J. Goodnow

Q14. 'Introduction to the Study of Public Administration' (1926), regarded as the first textbook of the discipline, was written by:

  1. A. Woodrow Wilson
  2. B. Luther Gulick
  3. C. W. F. Willoughby
  4. D. Leonard D. White
Answer: D. Leonard D. White

Q15. Henri Fayol presented how many principles of management in his theory of administration?

  1. A. Fourteen
  2. B. Ten
  3. C. Twelve
  4. D. Five
Answer: A. Fourteen

Q16. Fayol's principle of 'unity of command' means that an employee should receive orders from:

  1. A. any senior officer
  2. B. only one superior
  3. C. the board of directors
  4. D. two bosses at a time
Answer: B. only one superior

Q17. Fayol's 'scalar chain' refers to:

  1. A. the scale of salaries
  2. B. the chain of production
  3. C. the line of authority running from the top to the bottom of an organisation
  4. D. the span of control
Answer: C. the line of authority running from the top to the bottom of an organisation

Q18. Fayol's principle of 'esprit de corps' emphasises:

  1. A. strict discipline
  2. B. fair remuneration
  3. C. centralisation of authority
  4. D. team spirit and morale among employees
Answer: D. team spirit and morale among employees

Q19. 'The Principles of Scientific Management' (1911) was written by:

  1. A. Frederick W. Taylor
  2. B. Henri Fayol
  3. C. Max Weber
  4. D. Elton Mayo
Answer: A. Frederick W. Taylor

Q20. Taylor's scientific management is most closely associated with:

  1. A. group dynamics
  2. B. time and motion studies
  3. C. legal-rational authority
  4. D. self-actualisation
Answer: B. time and motion studies

Q21. In Weber's typology, authority that rests on the exceptional personal qualities of a leader is called:

  1. A. traditional authority
  2. B. legal authority
  3. C. charismatic authority
  4. D. bureaucratic authority
Answer: C. charismatic authority

Q22. The concept of the 'iron cage', describing the trapping of individuals in rationalised bureaucratic systems, is associated with:

  1. A. Karl Marx
  2. B. Vilfredo Pareto
  3. C. Emile Durkheim
  4. D. Max Weber
Answer: D. Max Weber

Q23. Max Weber, the theorist of bureaucracy, was a German:

  1. A. sociologist
  2. B. general
  3. C. statesman
  4. D. engineer
Answer: A. sociologist

Q24. The highest level of Maslow's hierarchy of needs is:

  1. A. esteem needs
  2. B. self-actualisation
  3. C. safety needs
  4. D. social needs
Answer: B. self-actualisation

Q25. In Herzberg's two-factor theory, salary and working conditions are examples of:

  1. A. motivators
  2. B. satisfiers
  3. C. hygiene factors
  4. D. esteem needs
Answer: C. hygiene factors
