Q1. The most common cause of myocardial infarction is:

  1. A. Infection
  2. B. Atherosclerotic plaque rupture with clot formation
  3. C. Vitamin deficiency
  4. D. Allergy
Answer: B. Atherosclerotic plaque rupture with clot formation

Q2. HbA1c reflects blood sugar control over approximately:

  1. A. 1 day
  2. B. 2-3 months
  3. C. 1 year
  4. D. 1 week
Answer: B. 2-3 months

Q3. The first-line drug for type 2 diabetes is usually:

  1. A. Insulin
  2. B. Metformin
  3. C. Glibenclamide
  4. D. Acarbose
Answer: B. Metformin

Q4. A patient with severe asthma attack who becomes silent (silent chest) is:

  1. A. Improving
  2. B. In a life-threatening state
  3. C. Sleeping
  4. D. Recovering
Answer: B. In a life-threatening state

Q5. The classic triad of diabetes mellitus is:

  1. A. Fever, cough, pain
  2. B. Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia
  3. C. Rash, itching, swelling
  4. D. Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea
Answer: B. Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia

Q6. Chronic kidney disease is most commonly caused by:

  1. A. Trauma
  2. B. Diabetes and hypertension
  3. C. Infection only
  4. D. Allergy
Answer: B. Diabetes and hypertension

Q7. The drug of choice for anaphylaxis is:

  1. A. Antihistamine
  2. B. Intramuscular adrenaline (epinephrine)
  3. C. Steroids
  4. D. Salbutamol
Answer: B. Intramuscular adrenaline (epinephrine)

Q8. Atrial fibrillation increases the risk of:

  1. A. Stroke from embolism
  2. B. Broken bones
  3. C. Blindness
  4. D. Hearing loss
Answer: A. Stroke from embolism

Q9. Peptic ulcer disease is most often linked to:

  1. A. Spicy food alone
  2. B. H. pylori infection and NSAID use
  3. C. Stress only
  4. D. Cold weather
Answer: B. H. pylori infection and NSAID use

Q10. The normal range for serum potassium is about:

  1. A. 2.0-3.0 mmol/L
  2. B. 3.5-5.0 mmol/L
  3. C. 5.5-7.0 mmol/L
  4. D. 135-145 mmol/L
Answer: B. 3.5-5.0 mmol/L

Q11. In the regulation of systemic arterial pressure, the kidneys contribute significantly through which of the following mechanisms?

  1. A. Controlling blood volume and releasing vasoactive hormones
  2. B. Producing clotting factors
  3. C. Storing excess glucose
  4. D. Filtering respiratory gases
Answer: A. Controlling blood volume and releasing vasoactive hormones

Q12. Which of the following clinical roles typically encompasses diagnosing, managing adult diseases, and coordinating primary medical care?

  1. A. An internist
  2. B. A radiologist
  3. C. An orthopedic specialist
  4. D. A pathologist
Answer: A. An internist

Q13. Besides fluid regulation and waste filtration, what other essential physiological function is directly influenced by the kidneys?

  1. A. Bile salt production
  2. B. Insulin secretion
  3. C. Red blood cell production
  4. D. Enzyme digestion
Answer: C. Red blood cell production

Q14. An internal medicine physician who primarily delivers comprehensive healthcare to adults and acts as their primary care provider is commonly referred to as:

  1. A. A pediatrician
  2. B. An internist
  3. C. A surgeon
  4. D. An obstetrician
Answer: B. An internist

Q15. What is the primary professional focus of a specialist in internal medicine regarding their patient demographic?

  1. A. Newborn infants exclusively
  2. B. Adult patients across a spectrum of health conditions
  3. C. Surgical procedures for trauma patients only
  4. D. Pediatric developmental disorders
Answer: B. Adult patients across a spectrum of health conditions

Q16. Which primary organ system is principally responsible for filtering metabolic waste products and regulating fluid and electrolyte balance in the human body?

  1. A. The kidneys
  2. B. The liver
  3. C. The lungs
  4. D. The spleen
Answer: A. The kidneys

Q17. Which of the following autoantibodies is most specific for diagnosing systemic lupus erythematosus?

  1. A. Anti-Scl-70
  2. B. Anti-Jo-1
  3. C. Anti-dsDNA
  4. D. Anti-Centromere
Answer: C. Anti-dsDNA

Q18. What is the hallmark spirometry finding in obstructive lung diseases such as COPD?

  1. A. Decreased FEV1/FVC ratio
  2. B. Increased total lung capacity
  3. C. Normal FEV1 with reduced FVC
  4. D. Decreased residual volume
Answer: A. Decreased FEV1/FVC ratio

Q19. Which heart valve abnormality is most commonly associated with a history of rheumatic fever?

  1. A. Aortic regurgitation
  2. B. Mitral stenosis
  3. C. Tricuspid regurgitation
  4. D. Pulmonic stenosis
Answer: B. Mitral stenosis

Q20. What is the primary mechanism of action of loop diuretics like furosemide?

  1. A. Inhibition of aldosterone receptors in the collecting duct
  2. B. Inhibition of the Na+-K+-2Cl- cotransporter in the thick ascending limb
  3. C. Blockade of sodium-chloride symporters in the distal convoluted tubule
  4. D. Osmotic water withdrawal in the proximal convoluted tubule
Answer: B. Inhibition of the Na+-K+-2Cl- cotransporter in the thick ascending limb

Q21. Which electrolyte imbalance is most frequently observed in patients with acute Addison's disease?

  1. A. Hypernatremia and hypokalemia
  2. B. Hyponatremia and hyperkalemia
  3. C. Hypernatremia and hyperkalemia
  4. D. Hyponatremia and hypokalemia
Answer: B. Hyponatremia and hyperkalemia

Q22. Which organism is the most frequent cause of community-acquired pneumonia in adults?

  1. A. Pseudomonas aeruginosa
  2. B. Staphylococcus aureus
  3. C. Streptococcus pneumoniae
  4. D. Legionella pneumophila
Answer: C. Streptococcus pneumoniae

Q23. Which electrolyte imbalance is most frequently observed in hospitalized patients with severe congestive heart failure?

  1. A. Hypernatremia
  2. B. Hyperkalemia
  3. C. Hyponatremia
  4. D. Hypercalcemia
Answer: C. Hyponatremia

Q24. Which organism is the most frequent cause of typical community-acquired pneumonia in adults?

  1. A. Mycoplasma pneumoniae
  2. B. Streptococcus pneumoniae
  3. C. Legionella pneumophila
  4. D. Pseudomonas aeruginosa
Answer: B. Streptococcus pneumoniae

Q25. What is the primary mechanism of action of loop diuretics such as furosemide?

  1. A. Inhibition of sodium-chloride cotransporters in the distal convoluted tubule
  2. B. Blockade of epithelial sodium channels in the collecting duct
  3. C. Inhibition of the sodium-potassium-2chloride cotransporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle
  4. D. Antagonism of aldosterone receptors in the renal cortex
Answer: C. Inhibition of the sodium-potassium-2chloride cotransporter in the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle
