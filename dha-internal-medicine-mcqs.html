Q1. The most common cause of myocardial infarction is:

  1. A. Infection
  2. B. Atherosclerotic plaque rupture with clot formation
  3. C. Vitamin deficiency
  4. D. Allergy
Answer: B. Atherosclerotic plaque rupture with clot formation

Q2. HbA1c reflects blood sugar control over approximately:

  1. A. 1 day
  2. B. 2-3 months
  3. C. 1 year
  4. D. 1 week
Answer: B. 2-3 months

Q3. The first-line drug for type 2 diabetes is usually:

  1. A. Insulin
  2. B. Metformin
  3. C. Glibenclamide
  4. D. Acarbose
Answer: B. Metformin

Q4. A patient with severe asthma attack who becomes silent (silent chest) is:

  1. A. Improving
  2. B. In a life-threatening state
  3. C. Sleeping
  4. D. Recovering
Answer: B. In a life-threatening state

Q5. The classic triad of diabetes mellitus is:

  1. A. Fever, cough, pain
  2. B. Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia
  3. C. Rash, itching, swelling
  4. D. Nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea
Answer: B. Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia

Q6. Chronic kidney disease is most commonly caused by:

  1. A. Trauma
  2. B. Diabetes and hypertension
  3. C. Infection only
  4. D. Allergy
Answer: B. Diabetes and hypertension

Q7. The drug of choice for anaphylaxis is:

  1. A. Antihistamine
  2. B. Intramuscular adrenaline (epinephrine)
  3. C. Steroids
  4. D. Salbutamol
Answer: B. Intramuscular adrenaline (epinephrine)

Q8. Atrial fibrillation increases the risk of:

  1. A. Stroke from embolism
  2. B. Broken bones
  3. C. Blindness
  4. D. Hearing loss
Answer: A. Stroke from embolism

Q9. Peptic ulcer disease is most often linked to:

  1. A. Spicy food alone
  2. B. H. pylori infection and NSAID use
  3. C. Stress only
  4. D. Cold weather
Answer: B. H. pylori infection and NSAID use

Q10. The normal range for serum potassium is about:

  1. A. 2.0-3.0 mmol/L
  2. B. 3.5-5.0 mmol/L
  3. C. 5.5-7.0 mmol/L
  4. D. 135-145 mmol/L
Answer: B. 3.5-5.0 mmol/L
